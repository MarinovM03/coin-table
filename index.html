How it works

  1. Your wallet holds coins, not a number. Each is an unspent output left by some earlier transaction.
  2. To pay, you spend whole coins. They become the transaction’s inputs. A coin can’t be snapped in half.
  3. The transaction makes new coins. One output pays them; another returns the rest to you as change.
  4. Whatever isn’t assigned is the fee. The miner who puts it in a block keeps it.
UTXOUnspent transaction output: an amount locked to a key you control. Your “balance” is just these added up.
InputA UTXO being spent. It’s always consumed whole.
OutputA new amount created by a transaction. Unspent, it’s someone’s UTXO.
ChangeAn output that pays you back. Usually sent to a fresh address in your own wallet.
FeeInputs minus outputs. There’s no fee line in a transaction — it’s the gap.
Sats1 bitcoin = 100,000,000 satoshis.
sat/vBFees are priced by size. More inputs → bigger transaction → bigger fee.

Sizes assume native SegWit (P2WPKH): ≈68 vB per input, 31 vB per output, about 10.5 vB overhead. Outputs under 294 sats are “dust” that nodes won’t relay by default, so when a change coin would end up that small (after paying for its own output), wallets skip it and the leftover goes to the fee. Coins, txids and the time-lapse block are demo data.
